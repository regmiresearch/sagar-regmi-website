I lead the robotics, automation, and AI laboratory at Quincy University, where I work on field robotics and machine perception for autonomous machines that operate in unstructured, real-world environments such as farms and industrial sites. I also teach mechanical and electrical engineering courses that connect theory with hands-on laboratories and design projects.

Research

My research interests include field robotics and machine perception, multi-agent planning and control, and sensing, instrumentation, and AI for autonomous machine systems in off-road, agricultural, and industrial settings.

Current project: Speed vs. power for robot vision on embedded hardware

This undergraduate research project measures how processing speed and power consumption trade off when running robot vision workloads on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano. The results will inform how edge-AI robots should be configured for battery-limited operation.

Earlier work: agricultural perception and machine systems

  • On-machine perception for tillage residue sensing with deep learning, with vehicle-mounted stereo and multispectral cameras and ResNet18 classification (industry-sponsored).
  • An open-source segmentation tool for crop residue, using U-Net and DeepLabV3 models for residue cover estimation.
  • Data-informed surface drainage and yield impact, using 13 years of georeferenced yield data (USDA-NIFA sponsored).
  • Combine harvester performance and grain loss distribution (industry-sponsored evaluation).
  • Low-density particleboards made from wheat straw and distillers dried grains with solubles.
